# Madison Reflections

I didn't expect much from the trip to Madison, but it turned out to be exactly what I needed.

I met Rod Walton, founder of Helpers, and he stood out immediately. No self-inflation, no performed humility — just direct, authentic, and sincere. That kind of presence is rare in startup circles, and I respected it. It reminded me of what actually draws people in: not the pitch, but the person behind it.

The trip also forced me to think honestly about direction. It's easy to stay busy without being focused — using tools without leveraging them, operating across multiple markets without committing to one, defaulting to a strategy because it sounds reasonable rather than because it's been pressure-tested. The hard part isn't doing more. It's narrowing down and going deep on the thing that actually matters.

I left Madison with a clearer sense of what needs to happen next: pick a lane, build the first thing well, and let everything else follow from that.
